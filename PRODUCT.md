# Product

## Register

product

## Users

In-house recruiters and hiring managers at one employer, reviewing a batch of up to 30 CVs for a single vacancy. They work at a desk in office daylight, usually in a focused 60 to 90 minute block, reading dense CV text and making decisions they may later need to justify to candidates, colleagues or an auditor. An administrator sets up vacancies and criteria; reviewers read every CV.

## Product Purpose

Shortlist helps a recruiter apply one published, 100-point rubric consistently to every CV in a batch. AI classifies evidence per criterion and quotes the source; a person reviews every CV, confirms or changes each judgement, and chooses up to three candidates. Scoring is deterministic. Success is a shortlist the recruiter can explain from the evidence, reached without skipping a CV and without the interface pushing a conclusion.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, trustworthy. Elegant through restraint rather than decoration. The voice is plain and specific: it names evidence, never judges people. It should feel like a well-made instrument a professional chooses, not a generated dashboard.

## Anti-references

- Generic "vibe-coded" SaaS: gradient heroes, glass cards, big-number KPI tiles, identical icon card grids, coloured side-stripe accents, emoji icons.
- Applicant-tracking clutter: dense pipelines, scorecards that rate people "strong/weak", dashboards for their own sake.
- Anything that implies AI certainty: confidence percentages, animated score counters, "match scores" for people.
- Chat interfaces for the core workflow.

## Design Principles

1. Evidence first. Every judgement sits next to its source passage and is one action away from it.
2. The person decides. AI output is labelled "AI note", is editable, and never confirms anything on its own.
3. One obvious next action per screen, and a stated reason whenever it is disabled.
4. Motion reports state and never makes a reviewer wait.
5. Familiar, consistent controls over novelty; delight is reserved for moments such as processing and finalisation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA minimum: 4.5:1 text contrast, 3:1 for controls and meaningful graphics, visible focus, full keyboard operation, Escape and focus restoration for overlays, labelled inputs. Status always uses text and an icon as well as colour. Layouts work at 390px and 200% zoom, and evidence and source panels stack on narrow screens. Reduced motion and reduced transparency preferences are honoured. Plain-language categories from the specification are used verbatim: "Full evidence", "Partial evidence", "Not evidenced in this CV", "Needs your judgement".
